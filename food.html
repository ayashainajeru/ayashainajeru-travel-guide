🍓 The Cultural Food of Baguio City 🍓

🍓 Strawberry Taho

Strawberry taho is a famous local twist on the classic Filipino soy-based snack.

Base Ingredients: It features the traditional components of warm, velvety silken tofu and chewy sago (tapioca) pearls.

The Twist:Instead of the standard arnibal (dark brown sugar caramel syrup), it is generously layered and topped with a vibrant pink-to-red strawberry-infused syrup and chunks of fresh local strawberries.

Flavor Vibe: It offers a sweet, fruity, and comforting balance that is a favorite to sip or spoon through during the cool highland breeze of Baguio.

Price: Commonly sold by street vendors walking along tourist spots, parks, and roadsides in clear plastic cups, typically costing around ₱20 to ₱50.

🌭 Baguio Longganisa

Baguio longganisa is a famous Filipino pork sausage originating from Baguio City.

✨ Main Varieties

De Recado (Garlic): The more traditional and popular profile, known for a bold, garlicky, and savory taste with a balanced ratio of lean pork and pork fat.

Hamonado (Sweet): A sweeter version seasoned with a rich, savory-sweet blend.

Hot / Piquante:A spiced, savory version crafted for those who prefer a little heat.

Quality: Made with a balanced mix of lean ground pork and pork fat, offering a firm yet juicy bite.

History: Production traces back to the 1940s and 1950s by foundational local families such as the Alabanzas, Tuveras, Alvar, Lapira, and the Dipasupil/Enriquez family.

Where to Find It: It is a classic pasalubong (souvenir food item) from the region and is widely available at the meat section of the Baguio Public Market.

🍲 Pinikpikan

Pinikpikan is a traditional, savory chicken soup or stew from the Cordillera region (including Baguio and surrounding highland communities) of the Philippines.

✨ Key Characteristics

The Name Process: Derived from the Ilocano word pikpik (to lightly tap, hit, or beat), the traditional preparation involves gently beating the chicken on its wings and body before cooking.

Smoking and Roasting:After slaughter, the chicken is put over open flames to burn off the feathers. This gives the skin a charred look and imparts a deep, smoky flavor.

Broth Ingredients: It is typically boiled with ginger, native spices, and etag (traditional Cordilleran smoked or salt-cured pork), and sometimes softened with vegetables like sayote.

Cultural Roots: Historically, pinikpikan was prepared as a sacred ritual by indigenous Igorot communities to honor ancestor spirits or the deity Kabunyan. Today, it is also served as a warm specialty in highland restaurants and homes.

💜 Good Shepherd Ube Jam

Good Shepherd Ube Jam (often called Mountain Maid Ube Jam) is a famous Filipino purple yam spread produced by the Religious of the Good Shepherd Convent in Baguio City.

✨ Key Characteristics

Appearance: A natural lavender-to-deep purple-hued spread housed in a clear glass bottle with the convent's logo.

Texture and Taste: Smooth, creamy, sticky, and melt-in-your-mouth. It features a moderate, balanced sweetness that highlights a strong, genuine purple yam flavor.

Ingredients: Made from real local purple yams sourced from farmers in Benguet and Ilocos, combined with milk and butter.

Shelf Life: Because it has no preservatives, an unopened jar typically lasts about one month.
